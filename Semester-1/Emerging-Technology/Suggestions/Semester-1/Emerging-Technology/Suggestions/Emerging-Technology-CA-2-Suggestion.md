# Part A: Important Short Questions (2 Marks)

## 1. What is Raw Data?

Raw data is unprocessed facts, observations, or values collected from a source before processing or analysis.

**Example:** A list of students' marks before calculating the average.

## 2. What is a Dataset?

A dataset is an organized collection of related data records or observations used for analysis.

**Example:** A table containing students' names, roll numbers, and marks.

## 3. What is Primary Data?

Primary data is data collected directly by a researcher or organization for a specific purpose.

**Examples:** Surveys, interviews, questionnaires, experiments, and observations.

## 4. What is Secondary Data?

Secondary data is data that has already been collected by another person or organization and is reused for analysis.

**Examples:** Published reports, research papers, government datasets, and existing databases.

## 5. What is Data Cleaning?

Data cleaning is the process of identifying and correcting or handling errors, missing values, duplicate records, and inconsistencies in a dataset.

**Example:** Correcting spelling mistakes and removing duplicate student records.

## 6. What is an Outlier?

An outlier is a data observation that differs significantly from the general pattern of other observations.

**Example:** In a dataset of salaries ranging from ₹10,000 to ₹30,000, a salary of ₹5,00,000 may be an outlier.

## 7. What is Data Visualization?

Data visualization is the graphical representation of data using charts, graphs, maps, or other visual forms.

**Examples:** Bar charts, histograms, pie charts, and scatter plots.

## 8. What is Qualitative Data?

Qualitative data is non-numerical data that describes qualities, characteristics, or categories.

**Examples:** Colour, gender category, product type, and customer feedback.

## 9. What is Quantitative Data?

Quantitative data is numerical data that can be counted or measured.

**Examples:** Age, height, weight, marks, and income.

## 10. What is Structured Data?

Structured data is data organized in a predefined format, usually in rows and columns.

**Examples:** Excel spreadsheets and relational databases.

## 11. What is Unstructured Data?

Unstructured data is data that does not follow a fixed tabular structure.

**Examples:** Images, videos, audio recordings, and free-text documents.

## 12. What is Data Privacy?

Data privacy refers to the appropriate collection, use, sharing, and governance of personal or sensitive information.

**Example:** Using a person's personal information only for authorized purposes.

## 13. What is Data Security?

Data security is the protection of data against unauthorized access, alteration, disclosure, damage, or loss.

**Examples:** Passwords, encryption, and access controls.

## 14. What is Data Quality?

Data quality refers to the degree to which data is accurate, complete, consistent, reliable, and suitable for its intended purpose.

## 15. What is Data Bias?

Data bias is a systematic distortion or under-representation or over-representation of certain groups or characteristics in data or its collection process.

**Example:** A dataset containing information mainly about urban students may not represent rural students accurately.

## 16. What is a Bar Chart?

A bar chart is a graphical representation that uses rectangular bars to compare values across different categories.

**Example:** Comparing the marks of students in different subjects.

## 17. What is a Histogram?

A histogram is a graph that shows the frequency distribution of numerical data across intervals or bins.

**Example:** Showing how many students scored within the ranges 0–20, 21–40, and 41–60.

## 18. What is a Scatter Plot?

A scatter plot is a graph that uses points to show the relationship between two numerical variables.

**Example:** Showing the relationship between study hours and examination marks.

## 19. Why is Data Literacy Important in AI?

Data literacy helps people understand, prepare, evaluate, and responsibly interpret the data used by Artificial Intelligence systems.

## 20. What is Data Interpretation?

Data interpretation is the process of explaining the meaning and significance of analyzed data in a particular context.

**Example:** Interpreting a graph to determine which subject has the highest average marks.

# Part B: Important Descriptive Questions (5 Marks)

## Q1. Explain the Importance of Data Literacy in Artificial Intelligence.

**Answer:**

Data literacy is the ability to read, understand, analyze, interpret, and communicate data effectively.

It is important in Artificial Intelligence for the following reasons:

1. **Understanding Data:** It helps people understand different types and sources of data.
2. **Data Preparation:** It helps identify missing values, errors, and duplicate records.
3. **Better Decisions:** It supports informed decision-making based on evidence.
4. **Evaluating AI Models:** It helps evaluate the quality and reliability of AI predictions.
5. **Identifying Bias:** It helps detect unfair representation in datasets.
6. **Responsible Use:** It promotes privacy, security, and ethical use of data.

**Conclusion:** Data literacy is essential for building reliable, fair, and responsible AI systems.

## Q2. Differentiate Between Primary Data and Secondary Data.

**Answer:**

| Basis | Primary Data | Secondary Data |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Data collected directly for a specific purpose. | Data previously collected by someone else. |
| Source | Original source or direct collection. | Existing reports, records, or datasets. |
| Methods | Surveys, interviews, and experiments. | Research papers, reports, and databases. |
| Cost | Usually more expensive to collect. | Often less expensive to obtain. |
| Time | Usually requires more time. | Usually quicker to obtain. |
| Example | Conducting a survey of students. | Using published student statistics. |

**Conclusion:** Primary data is collected first-hand, whereas secondary data is reused from existing sources.

## Q3. Explain Primary and Secondary Data and Data Collection Methods.

**Answer:**

### Primary Data

Primary data is collected directly by a researcher or organization for a specific purpose.

**Examples:** Surveys, interviews, questionnaires, experiments, and observations.

### Secondary Data

Secondary data is data already collected by another person or organization and reused for analysis.

**Examples:** Published reports, government datasets, research papers, and existing databases.

### Common Data Collection Methods

1. **Survey/Questionnaire:** Collects information from people through prepared questions.
2. **Interview:** Collects detailed information through direct conversation.
3. **Observation:** Collects data by observing people, events, or activities.
4. **Experiment:** Collects data under controlled conditions to study cause and effect.
5. **Sensors and IoT Devices:** Automatically collect measurements such as temperature and humidity.
6. **Transaction/Database Records:** Obtain information from sales, payments, registrations, and other records.
7. **Public or Open Datasets:** Reuse data made publicly available by governments or other organizations.

**Conclusion:** The choice of collection method depends on the purpose, accuracy requirements, cost, and availability of data.

## Q4. Differentiate Between Structured, Semi-Structured, and Unstructured Data.

**Answer:**

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Structured Data | Organized in a fixed format, usually rows and columns. | Excel tables, relational databases. |
| Semi-Structured Data | Contains tags, keys, or metadata but does not follow a strict tabular structure. | JSON, XML, emails. |
| Unstructured Data | Does not follow a fixed tabular structure. | Images, videos, audio, free text. |

### Explanation

**Structured Data:** It follows a predefined schema and is generally easy to query using database tools.

**Semi-Structured Data:** It contains organizational elements such as tags or key-value pairs, but its structure may vary between records.

**Unstructured Data:** It has no fixed tabular schema and often requires additional processing before analysis.

**Conclusion:** These three types differ mainly in how their information is organized.

## Q5. Explain Data by Modality with Examples.

**Answer:**

Data modality refers to the form in which information is represented.

The four important modalities are:

1. **Text Data:** Natural-language content such as emails, reviews, chat transcripts, and documents.
2. **Image Data:** Pixel-based visual information such as photographs, scanned documents, and satellite images.
3. **Audio Data:** Sound-based information such as speech recordings, music, and environmental sounds.
4. **Log Data:** Machine-generated, timestamped records of system or user events, such as server access logs and application logs.

**Example:** A customer-service interaction may generate a chat transcript (text), a screenshot (image), a recorded call (audio), and a timestamped ticket record (log).

**Conclusion:** Understanding data modalities helps select appropriate methods for data storage, processing, and analysis.

## Q6. Explain Data Cleaning and Its Importance.

**Answer:**

Data cleaning is the process of identifying and correcting or appropriately handling errors, missing values, duplicate records, and inconsistencies in a dataset.

### Common Data Quality Problems

1. **Missingness:** Expected values are absent from the dataset.
2. **Bias:** Data systematically misrepresents a population or phenomenon.
3. **Noise:** Random errors or fluctuations that do not represent the true signal.
4. **Duplicates:** The same record appears more than once.
5. **Inconsistency:** Different records use conflicting formats or values.

### Importance of Data Cleaning

- Improves data accuracy and completeness.
- Reduces errors during analysis.
- Helps produce more reliable results.
- Improves the quality of AI models.
- Supports better decision-making.

**Conclusion:** Clean data is essential for trustworthy analysis and reliable AI systems.

## Q7. Explain Missingness, Bias, and Noise with Their Basic Detection Methods.

**Answer:**

| Problem | Definition | Basic Detection Method |
|---|---|---|
| Missingness | Absence of expected values, such as blank cells or null fields. | Missingness heatmap or bar chart of null counts per column. |
| Bias | Systematic skew in data that misrepresents a population or phenomenon. | Compare histograms or bar charts across demographic or category subgroups. |
| Noise | Random, non-systematic errors or fluctuations unrelated to the true signal. | Scatter plots and box plots to examine spread and potential outliers. |

### Explanation

**Missingness:** Check which columns contain blank or null values and determine how to handle them.

**Bias:** Compare how different groups are represented in the dataset to identify possible under-representation or over-representation.

**Noise:** Examine unusual fluctuations and potential outliers to understand whether the data contains random errors.

**Conclusion:** Detecting these problems helps improve data quality and the reliability of analysis.

## Q8. Differentiate Between Data Privacy and Data Security.

**Answer:**

| Basis | Data Privacy | Data Security |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Concerns appropriate collection, use, and sharing of personal data. | Concerns protection of data against unauthorized access and damage. |
| Main Focus | How data should be collected and used. | How data should be protected. |
| Examples | Consent, purpose limitation, and responsible data sharing. | Encryption, passwords, and access controls. |
| Goal | Protect individuals' information rights. | Maintain confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data. |

**Conclusion:** Privacy governs appropriate data use, while security protects data from threats. Both are necessary for responsible data management.

## Q9. Explain Data Visualization and Its Importance.

**Answer:**

Data visualization is the graphical representation of information using charts, graphs, maps, and other visual forms.

### Importance

1. Makes complex data easier to understand.
2. Helps identify patterns and trends.
3. Supports comparison between categories.
4. Helps identify unusual values and possible outliers.
5. Supports data interpretation and decision-making.
6. Improves communication of analytical findings.

**Examples:** Bar charts, histograms, scatter plots, and line charts.

**Conclusion:** Data visualization converts data into a form that is easier to understand and communicate.

## Q10. Differentiate Between a Bar Chart, Histogram, and Scatter Plot.

**Answer:**

| Chart | Purpose | Data/Use | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bar Chart | Compares values across categories. | Categorical data. | Comparing marks in different subjects. |
| Histogram | Shows the distribution of numerical values across intervals. | Numerical data grouped into bins. | Distribution of students' marks. |
| Scatter Plot | Shows the relationship between two numerical variables. | Paired numerical data. | Study hours versus examination marks. |

**Conclusion:** The appropriate chart depends on the type of data and the question being investigated.

## Q11. Explain Data Quality and Its Main Characteristics.

**Answer:**

Data quality refers to how suitable and reliable data is for its intended purpose.

The main characteristics are:

1. **Accuracy:** Data correctly represents the real-world value.
2. **Completeness:** Required values are present.
3. **Consistency:** Data does not contain conflicting information.
4. **Validity:** Data follows the required format and rules.
5. **Uniqueness:** Unnecessary duplicate records are absent.
6. **Timeliness:** Data is sufficiently up-to-date for its purpose.

**Conclusion:** High-quality data leads to more reliable analysis and better decisions.

## Q12. Explain Data Bias and Its Effects on AI.

**Answer:**

Data bias is a systematic distortion in a dataset that can cause it to misrepresent a population or phenomenon.

### Causes of Data Bias

1. Unrepresentative sampling.
2. Under-representation of certain groups.
3. Incorrect data collection methods.
4. Historical inequalities reflected in existing records.
5. Incorrect labeling or measurement.

### Effects on AI

- Produces inaccurate predictions.
- May lead to unfair outcomes.
- Reduces the reliability of AI systems.
- Can disadvantage under-represented groups.

### Ways to Reduce Bias

- Use representative datasets.
- Examine subgroup representation.
- Review data collection and labeling methods.
- Evaluate model performance across different groups.

**Conclusion:** Identifying and reducing data bias is important for fair and reliable AI systems.
